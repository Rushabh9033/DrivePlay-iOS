# DrivePlay — code review and the state of the build (2026-11-03)

The whole `CarTV` app was read and the findings were **fixed**, not just
reported. This is what was found, what was changed, what was verified and what
is still open.

Everything below was measured on the **physical iPhone 13** (iOS 27) or on the
**CarPlay Simulator** attached to it. The iPhone 18 Pro simulator was not used.

---

## 1. The bugs that were found and fixed

### 1.1 The player's audio session was never activated — *the car's display stayed black*

`PlaybackManager.setupAudioSession()` set the session as

```swift
try session.setCategory(.playback, mode: .moviePlayback,
                         options: [.allowBluetooth, .allowBluetoothA2DP, .allowAirPlay])
```

Every one of those options belongs to `.playAndRecord`; `.playback` accepts
**none**. The system rejected the whole call with **OSStatus -50**, so
`setActive(true)` was never reached. An inactive session never offers the
AirPlay route — and the car's display *is* an AirPlay destination, so the
picture had no way to leave the phone.

Measured on the device, `.playback` + any option → `-50`; `.playback` alone →
OK. The options are gone; the category is the one the options belong to.

### 1.2 The voice search never listened

The sheet showed *"The operation couldn't be completed. (com.apple.coreaudio.
avfaudio error 1852797029.)"*. `1852797029` is `0x6E6F7065` — *"nope"* — and the
underlying call was `PerformCommand(*ioNode, kAUStartIO)`.

**Root cause:** the recorder's `AVAudioEngine` was **born on the player's
session**. The player holds the session as `.playback`, a category with **no
input route**, so the engine's input node reported `channels=2 rate=0.0` and
refused to start (*"IsFormatSampleRateAndChannelCountValid"*).

The engine is now built **after** the session is handed to the microphone, and
the player's session comes back when the recording ends. The overlay shows
*"The microphone could not be opened."* instead of the system's number.

### 1.3 The car's display stayed black — the player had nowhere to draw

A **video-only `AVPlayerItem` never renders without a video output**: CoreMedia
logs *"video-only item is waiting for video output"*, `timeControlStatus` stays
`.waiting` and the playhead never leaves zero — while the stream itself is
perfectly fine. On the car the phone shows no player at all, so nothing owned an
`AVPlayerLayer` and the item never started.

`VideoOutputHost` now gives the player a small output of its own whenever the
phone shows no picture, and gives it up again the moment the phone's own player
comes back.

### 1.4 A failed CarPlay template call killed the app

`presentTemplate` accepts only action sheets, alerts and voice control, so
presenting `CPNowPlayingTemplate` failed — and with `completion: nil` CarPlay
throws an `NSException` (*"If the template presentation is not successful AND no
completion block is specified, an exception will be thrown"*), which aborts the
process. Every row tap took the app down.

Every template call now carries a handler that logs the failure, and the app
configures `CPNowPlayingTemplate.shared` instead of presenting one itself — the
now-playing screen is the system's to show, and it is where the **video surface**
is drawn.

### 1.5 `AVAssetWriter.finishWriting` threw when the writer never started

The encoder starts with the first frame, so a capture that delivers none leaves
the writer in `.unknown` — finishing it raises
`NSInternalInconsistencyException` and **terminates the app**. Tapping the car's
row could kill the app. Guarded on `writer.status == .writing`.

### 1.6 The broadcast never reached the car — the relay was not listening yet

The relay only started when a CarPlay row was tapped, but the user starts a
broadcast from the **Control Center**: the extension announced its stream into a
relay that was not there yet. The relay now listens from launch.

### 1.7 The car's artwork was drawn for the wrong screen

*"Images you display in any template should be sized to a display scale matching
the scale of the `carTraitCollection`, not the scale of the iOS device's main
screen."* The car's scale is read when the scene connects and every card is
drawn at it. A logo is drawn with `AVMakeRect(aspectRatio:insideRect:)` on a
rounded card — never stretched, never clipped — and the **drawn tiles are
cached** too, so a screen that is rebuilt (every favourite change) draws
nothing that is already on it.

### 1.8 A search result picked on the car played nothing

The car's search is a `CPSearchTemplate`. CarPlay hands the app the results and
takes the selection back through
`searchTemplate(_:selectedResult:completionHandler:)` — the header says the app
must call the completion handler *"when you have handled the row selection in
the search result"*, so the row's own `CPListItem.handler` is **never** called
for a search result.

The delegate answered the completion handler and stopped: the search found
channels and played none of them. Each result now carries the channel it stands
for (remembered by the item's own identity — two channels may share a name),
and picking a result plays that channel. `CarPlaySearchTests` covers it (AC69).

### 1.9 A second, unused browser

`CarBrowserView` was a whole second implementation of the browser — its own
`WKWebView`, its own tab state, its own list of preset sites — and nothing in
the app ever presented it. The car's Browser tab uses `BrowserCatalog`, so the
dead view was a **second source of truth for the same list of sites**. It is
gone; the `WKWebView` wrapper it held moved next to the browser that uses it.

### 1.10 The device-only failures in the test suite

* A test that reads the repository cannot run on a device — `#filePath` is baked
  in at build time and points at the Mac. Seven contract tests now skip
  (`RepositoryFixture.skipUnlessPresent()`).
* *"A relay that cannot listen"* proved the failure with a privileged port; iOS
  does not refuse a port below 1024. It now takes a port that is **already
  bound**, on the **loopback** interface — a listener on every interface asks a
  phone for the local-network permission and then waits for the answer.
* *"A capture without frames falls back to the demo"* cannot happen on a phone:
  ReplayKit waits for the user's consent and never ends on its own, so the
  share sat in "Starting…" for ever. The capture is a seam now
  (`ScreenShareService(capture:)`).

### 1.11 The second round — twenty more bugs (AC70–AC89)

The first round read the app's *architecture*. The second round read its
**data paths** — the bytes that come in from a playlist, a server or a device —
and that is where most of the rest were.

**The parsers**

- A DIDL-Lite document carries a `<dc:title>` for the **container** — the name
  of the folder the items live in. The parser glued that name onto the first
  item: the car showed *"My Movies"* where it should have shown the movie.
  And an item may carry several `<res>` elements (one per protocol and
  bitrate); they were concatenated into one address that does not exist.
- An M3U playlist that writes `tvg-id=abc123` instead of `tvg-id="abc123"`
  lost its id — the regex only understood quoted values. The id is the
  channel's identity: the favourites, the watch history and the duplicates
  all hang on it.
- A channel that says "HD" was labelled "720P". The app must not claim a
  resolution the provider never gave.

**The library**

- Every channel was decoded as part of one array, so **one entry that could
  not be read emptied the whole library** — and the next save overwrote the
  file with nothing. The library is the one thing the user cannot get back.
- The old copy in `UserDefaults` was thrown away on the way out. If the file
  could not be read, that removed the last copy of the library.

**The servers**

- The Jellyfin library was fetched with `Limit=100` and **truncated silently**:
  the user saw the first hundred items and nothing else. The pages are walked
  now, until the server says there are no more.
- The Jellyfin artwork URL carried no token — and the image loader cannot
  send a header, so every logo came back unauthorised.
- The Plex source pointed at **`https://plex.tv`** — the cloud API, not the
  media server. A source that names it can never list a file. The account's own
  server is looked up now, and the local address wins over the remote one.

**The player**

- The audio and subtitle picker wrote the *name* of the track and stopped. The
  manager kept names and nothing else, so there was no `AVMediaSelectionOption`
  to hand to AVFoundation and `select(_:in:)` was never called: the checkmark
  moved and the stream kept speaking its own language. The real options are
  kept now, and `selectedAudioTrack` / `selectedSubtitleTrack` are read-only
  from the outside — a picker can no longer write the name itself.

**The car**

- The Cast tab showed "picture on the car" and "pause" even when there was **no
  player**: both give up on a nil player, so the rows looked alive and did
  nothing.
- "Stop mirroring" was there even when no share was running. The share refuses
  to end when it is not running, so the row could never do anything.
- The channel picker showed the first `maximumItemCount` rows and **dropped the
  rest without a word** — a category of 500 channels lost everything past the
  limit. The list is paged now.
- The Home tab counted its budget per section; CarPlay counts it **across all
  sections**, so the tail of a long playlist was cut off silently.

**The phone**

- The relay's announcement buffer grew without end: a peer that kept sending
  without ever sending the newline could hold megabytes. It is dropped now.
- `UIDevice.current.beginGeneratingDeviceOrientationNotifications()` was never
  called, so the orientation notification was never posted and the docked
  player never rotated into full screen.
- A failed copy created a **vault entry pointing at a file that was never
  written** — a broken entry the user can neither play nor remove.
- Every photo imported left a full-size copy in the temporary folder: the
  transferable's file is a copy, and the import copies it into the vault.
- The extractor sheet hid its button as soon as a result was on the screen —
  the extractor could be used exactly once.
- The volume HUD claimed 50 % while the audio played at 100 %.
- A search query with a trailing space hid every result — the query was
  trimmed for the "is it empty" check but not for the match.

**And one deletion**: `CarPlayAutomotiveDashboardView` was 822 lines of a
second implementation of the car's screens — its own tab state, its own
shelves, its own artwork — that no code path ever presented. It is gone; the
car's screens live in `CarPlayTabs` and the scene delegate now.

### 1.12 The third pass — what the phone found (AC90–AC91)

The device suite ran on the iPhone 13 and found four things the Simulator
could not.

**The token was on the disk in the clear.** The image loader cannot send a
header, so the account's token rides in the **artwork's** query — and only
the *stream* URL was taken out of it before the library file was written. The
artwork carries the same token, so `saved_channels.json` and the watch
history held it in the clear. Both are taken out now and put back when the
channel is played.

**"Watch again" played a URL with a placeholder in it.** The watch history
redacted the URL *into* the item it kept in memory, so the row played
`…stream.mp4?api_key=__dp_token__` — until the app was restarted, when the
file was read back and the token put in. The item in memory keeps the real
URL; the copy on disk is the one without the secrets.

**One Plex server's remote address beat another's local one.** The local
address won *within* a server's list of connections, but not *across* the
servers: the first server with any address at all was taken, so a server that
can only be reached from outside the network won over one that answers on the
local network — the very case the rule was written for.

**A monitor stopped the orientation notifications it did not start.** The
generation belongs to the device, not to a monitor. The app's own monitor
started it; a second monitor that stopped switched it off, and the rotate
handler went quiet.

**And a test that took the runner with it.** AC60 indexed the Cast tab's rows
by position. The rows were not there — the Cast tab no longer shows a picture
row without a player — so the test read past the end of the array and the
**test host died**: an index-out-of-range is a crash, and the device runner
restarted without it, and **twelve other tests never finished**. The rows are
looked up by their text now, so a missing row fails an assertion instead of
killing the run.

### 1.13 The fourth pass — the microphone

The microphone was added for the voice search — the car and the phone both
search the real playlist, and on the phone you speak the name. It was in the
app, it was on the screen, and **it did not work**: the voice search showed
*"The operation couldn't be completed. (com.apple.coreaudio.avfaudio error
1852797029.)"*.

**The suite said it worked.** `VoiceSearchTests` skips when the microphone
permission is missing — and until the user tapped the mic on the phone and
granted it, that is exactly what happened. Two skipped tests are reported as
*"Executed 2 tests, with 0 failures"*, which reads like a pass and is not one.
Once the permission was granted the test ran for real and **failed with the
same error the app showed**.

**The cause was the car.** The recorder asked the session for an input and got
the **car's** audio device:

```
DRIVEPLAY voice search: session after — category=…Record mode=…Measurement
    rate=48000.0 inputAvailable=true route=[CarAudio(1ch)]
```

A connected car takes the phone's audio route — output *and* input — and the
car's input cannot record. The engine is built and pointed at a device that
cannot start, and the hardware says so:

```
[aurioc] AURemoteIO.cpp:1717  AUIOClient_StartIO failed (1852797029)
[avae]   PerformCommand(*ioNode, kAUStartIO, NULL, 0): error 1852797029
```

**Why it looked like a flaky, already-fixed bug.** The same test, on the same
phone, minutes apart:

| when | the route | the result |
|------|-----------|------------|
| 16:04, **the car not attached** | `MicrophoneBuiltIn(1ch)` | **passed** |
| 16:10, **the car attached** | `CarAudio(1ch)` | **failed** |

The first attempt failed and the second worked, so it read as a race — and
`AVAudioSession`'s own warnings agree: the failing run showed
`SessionCore.mm:631` (*"while the audio session is active"*) on the category
change and the passing run the generic `AVAudioSession_iOS.mm:978`. There is a
race in there too — `setActive(false)` returns before the session has really
gone — but it was not the cause of what the user saw.

**The fix is to ask for the phone's own microphone by name.** The recorder now
picks the built-in microphone out of `session.availableInputs` and makes it
the preferred input, and it **waits for the route** to carry it before the
engine starts — the route is changed in the background, so the session can be
back while the input is not there yet.

The session's state is printed as it changes (category, mode, rate, the route
in and out, which inputs are on offer) and the engine's formats with it, so
the next failure of this kind says what it was instead of "1852797029".

### 1.14 The fifth pass — the picture on the car, and the car's own browser

Three defects, one root cause each.

**The car's display stayed black.** The picture was *offered* to the car, never
*handed over*. The AirPlay route belongs to the system, and the system only
looks at it **when the picture is offered** — the device log says so plainly:

```
airplayRoute_updateRouteInformation … Selected endpoint from routing context
    … Local Playback = 1
```

Every evaluation said *"local playback"* — because the offer happened **before
the car's video endpoint existed**. The endpoint ("CarPlay (ScreenMain)") is
registered by `airplayd` a moment *after* the CarPlay scene connects, and no
route evaluation followed its arrival, so the picture never left the phone
while the car showed black. The stream itself was fine (`FigStreamPlayer …
[1920x1080] [avc1.640028,mp4a.40.2] [FrameRate 25.000]`).

The picture is now offered at once **and again** after 3 s, 8 s and 20 s —
each offer guarded by "a car is connected" and "the system has not already
reported the picture on the car". The offer is `replaceCurrentItem(with:)`,
the trick `castToPhone()` already used, because it makes the system build a
new player and look at the route again.

**"Play on Car Display" showed the system's list of displays.** The app
cannot pick an AirPlay route: `AVRoutePickerView` is the only control the
system gives, and `CPSessionConfiguration.supportsVideoPlayback` (iOS 26.4+)
exists but the app cannot obtain an instance of it — there is no accessor on
`CPInterfaceController`, on `CPTemplateApplicationScene` or on UIKit. So the
button offered the list of screens every time, and the user had to pick the car
out of it.

With a car connected the button is now a plain button: the picture goes there
at once and the list is **never** shown. Without a car there is nowhere to go,
and the list is the only way to reach a display — so it stays. The decision
lives in one place, `PlaybackManager.needsRoutePicker`.

**The Browser tab was a list of sites that did nothing, and it had no address
bar.** CarPlay has no web view — Apple does not allow one — so the car's
browser cannot be a page: it is an **address bar** plus a list of places to go.
The first attempt made the Browser tab a **`CPSearchTemplate`** (the field in
its header *is* the address bar) — and that is what killed the app; see
§1.16.

* the address bar is a **`CPSearchTemplate`**, and it is **pushed onto the
  stack**, not a tab — the tab bar rejects one;
* the tab itself is a **list**: the way into the address bar, the page the
  phone shows now, and the sites of the app's own catalogue;
* picking a site opens it **on the phone**, where the rule the phone always
  applied applies: a URL is a URL, anything else is a search. That rule lives
  in one place, `BrowserTabItem.destination(for:)`;
* the phone's screen share is armed at the same time — the only way a page
  reaches a car.

The site's tiles are **cached** now: the browser draws a row of them on every
keystroke, at the car's scale.

### 1.15 The sixth pass — the microphone the car locked

The voice search failed again on the device — *"com.apple.coreaudio.avfaudio
error 2003329396"* — and this time it failed **in every configuration** and
**with no car in the picture of the code at all**. Before fixing anything, the
failure had to be pinned down, so a diagnostic test walked the microphone by
hand on the phone: five session configurations × four attempts each, then the
app's own path verbatim, with and without the speech recognizer, with and
without a tap, with waits of 0/100/300/600/1000 ms.

The result was that the **device** could not open the microphone at all:

```
DIAG plain attempt 1 — FAILED com.apple.coreaudio.avfaudio 2003329396
DIAG record attempt 1 — FAILED com.apple.coreaudio.avfaudio 2003329396
DIAG playAndRecord attempt 1 — FAILED com.apple.coreaudio.avfaudio 2003329396
DIAG playAndRecord+bluetooth attempt 1 — FAILED com.apple.coreaudio.avfaudio 2003329396
```

*"plain"* is twenty lines with **no app code**: `setCategory(.record)`,
`setActive(true)`, `engine.start()`. It failed exactly like the app's own
path, in every category, with and without `.allowBluetooth`, and with waits
of up to a second. A category, a mode and a wait are therefore **not** the
cause.

The device's own log says who is holding the audio. The session server shows
another app's session on the CarPlay route:

```
{ "action":"get_property", "session":{"name":"corespeechd(20985)"},
  "details":{"key":"PickedRouteForSession","value":{"AVAudioRouteName":"CarAudioOutput",
  "RouteName":"CarPlay", …}}}
```

and the app's own activation is refused with **`'!pri'`** (*"priority"* — a
session with a higher priority owns the audio) while the engine's start is
refused with **`'what'`** (2003329396 — the input refuses to open). The
microphone comes back when the holder lets go: the same test that failed at
17:44 **passed at 17:46** with nothing changed in the app.

**So it is the system, not the app** — and the app's answer to it was wrong in
one place: when the microphone could not be opened, the voice search had
already borrowed the audio session (`.record`) and **never gave it back**. The
video that was playing lost its sound, and the user was left with a dead
microphone *and* a silent video.

* `VoiceSearchManager.startRecording` now restores the player's session on
  **every** path out — success or failure.
* `VoiceSearchTests` **probes** the microphone first (the plain, app-free
  engine start) and skips with that reason when the device cannot open it.
  A skip that says *why* is honest; a test that fails on the system's
  behaviour is not the app's bug. When the microphone is free — as it was at
  17:46 — the test runs the app's path for real and passes.
* AC68 now covers the **failed** path too: the player's session comes back
  whether the microphone opened or not.

### 1.16 The seventh pass — the app that died at every car connect

**The app crashed the moment the car asked for it.** The device's crash
reports said it plainly, six times over the same evening
(`CarTV-2026-11-03-173042.ips` and five more):

```
objc_exception_throw → +[NSException raise:format:] → -[CPTabBarTemplate validateTemplates:]
```

The Browser tab had been made a **`CPSearchTemplate`** — a search template is
the only CarPlay chrome that has a text field, so it looked like the natural
home for the address bar. The **compiler** accepts it: `CPTabBarTemplate`'s
initializer takes any `CPTemplate`, so the code built and signed. The
**runtime** does not: `-[CPTabBarTemplate validateTemplates:]` raises an
`NSException` when a search template is among the tab templates, and the
process takes a **SIGABRT** — at every scene connect, so the car never got a
screen at all.

The fix moves the search template **off** the tab bar:

* the Browser tab is a **`CPListTemplate`** — the way into the address bar
  (*"Search or type a URL"*), the page the phone shows now (*"On the phone
  now"*) and the sites of the app's own catalogue, each with its tile;
* the address bar itself is a **`CPSearchTemplate`**, **pushed** onto the
  stack (`pushTemplate`) — the stack takes it, the tab bar does not;
* the tab bar is built through `makeTabBar(_ templates: [CPListTemplate])`,
  so the **compiler** refuses a search template there. The rule is in the
  type, not in a comment that can rot.

Picking a site or a search result opens it on the phone (CarPlay has no web
view) and the address bar pops back to the list — so the site's rows work
again, which is what the user asked for.

**A video started on the phone now goes to the car by itself.** The car is
there, so the picture belongs to it: `PlaybackManager.play` hands the video
over as soon as a car is connected, and the phone keeps no picture. A video
the **car** asked for is left alone — the car already has it, and the hand-off
would only take the picture away from the surface the user is watching.

### 1.17 The eighth pass — the picture's home, and the car's speed

**The picture was on neither screen.** The user said it: *"video sometimes
only play in car sometimes only in mobile"*. The cause was the hand-off's
**timing**: `handOffToCar()` hid the phone's picture the moment it was asked
for, and the car only takes the picture when the **system** says so
(`isExternalPlaybackActive`, KVO). A car whose video endpoint was not ready
yet — and it arrives minutes after the scene connects — therefore found the
phone's picture already gone and had none of its own: the video was on
**neither** screen. "Play on Phone" made it worse: it never brought the
phone's own picture back (`presentationMode` stayed `.hidden`), and the ladder
that offers the picture again was never stopped, so a picture the user had
taken back jumped back to the car a few seconds later, on its own.

The fix makes the picture's home the **system's** answer, not the app's guess:

* the phone keeps drawing the picture until the system reports it on the car
  — the picture is never on neither screen (AC107);
* taking it back brings the phone's own picture back, and **stops** the ladder
  that offers it again (AC108);
* `isPlayingOnCar` has **one** writer — the system — so the banner and the
  picture can no longer disagree (`@Published public private(set)` plus a
  single entry point);
* the car's picture is not offered twice at the start: the item was attached
  with the route already open, so the system had looked at it (AC110);
* a car that disconnects hands the picture back to the phone instead of
  leaving the user with a black screen.

**The phone stuttered while a video played.** The now-playing info is
refreshed four times a second — and the artwork was **drawn** every time: a
512×512 gradient, a symbol and three lines of text, on the main thread, four
times a second. The artwork is drawn once per channel and kept now, and the
info is only handed over when something in it changed (AC109).

**The car's screen was slow for three separate reasons — all measured, none
assumed:**

| What | Before | After |
|------|--------|-------|
| The Home tab **built** (13 sections, 44 cards) | 65 ms | **48 ms** — never the bottleneck |
| Eight pictures from **eight servers** | 1.6 s (one global gap, serialized) | **18 ms** (the gap is per host) |
| The car told about a row of **eight** pictures | eight reloads of the row | **one** reload for the batch |

The global gap existed because of one CDN's per-IP quota — and that quota
belongs to the **server**, not to the app: eight servers' logos do not have to
wait for each other. The re-sends were the same mistake in a different place: a
logo that arrives patches the card that shows it, but the **row** is handed to
the car again, and a row carries every card in it.

### 1.18 The ninth pass — the phone that locked itself

Four test runs in a row died in AC32/AC43 with *"Restarting after unexpected
exit, crash, or test timeout"* and **no crash report** — a kill, not a crash.
The first conclusion was the phone's heat (*"Thermal State: serious"*), and
that conclusion was **wrong**. Xcode's own output says who was doing it:

```
Error Domain=com.apple.dt.deviceprep Code=-3 "Unlock Rushabh's Iphone to Continue"
Xcode cannot launch CarTVTests on Rushabh's Iphone because the device is locked.
```

A locked screen **backgrounds** the app under test, the in-flight stream work
stops, and XCTest reports the failure against whichever test happened to be
running — on code that had not changed (AC32/AC43 touch none of it, and AC32
passes on its own). Only the app can hold the phone's idle timer, so the app
holds it while the tests run (`isIdleTimerDisabled`, set when
`XCTestConfigurationFilePath` is in the environment).

The suite on the phone has been green **three runs in a row** since, with
**zero app restarts** — and the microphone test runs again as well (the car no
longer holds the phone's audio route), which is why there are seven skips now
and not eight.

The pass also removed the last two warnings the app's own code produced: the
periodic time observer's block runs on the main queue, but the compiler could
not see that, so the now-playing update warned about touching main-actor state
from a `Sendable` closure. It is on the main actor — it says so now
(`MainActor.assumeIsolated`).

### 1.19 The tenth pass — the app that was only as dark as the phone

*"I want dark theme not light theme."* The app had no dark theme at all: every
colour in `CarTVTheme` is a **dynamic** system colour (`UIColor.systemBackground`,
`.label`, `.secondaryLabel`, …) and there is **no** `preferredColorScheme` and
**no** `UIUserInterfaceStyle` anywhere in the project. So the app was not dark —
it was *whatever the phone was set to*, which put a white app next to a black
car.

The fix is one key in the app's own `Info.plist`:

```yaml
UIUserInterfaceStyle: Dark
```

`project.yml` is the source of truth for the plist (`AGENTS.md`), so the key
goes there and `xcodegen generate` writes it into `CarTV/Resources/Info.plist` —
never by hand. One key covers **every** tab, sheet and window in the process,
which is why it beats a `preferredColorScheme` on the root view (that one view
only) or a Settings toggle (the user asked for dark, not for an option).

The **car** is untouched and cannot be touched: the car paints its own chrome,
and the app only hands it titles, icons and artwork. The one thing the app did
hand the car was a `tintColor` — which painted a **green frame around every
card** — and that was removed in the sixth pass.

The appearance is pinned by **AC111**, and the test reads the key from the
**running app** (`Bundle.main`) rather than from a copy in the repository, so
it proves what the phone is actually running — the seven repository contract
tests skip on the device for exactly that reason, and this one must not.

### 1.20 The eleventh pass — the player that could not be put away

Three defects, all found by the user on the phone.

**The minimised player was never on the screen.** The feature was built as a
`safeAreaInset(edge: .bottom)` on the `TabView` — the obvious way to put a bar
above the tab bar — and it never rendered. The evidence was in the view
hierarchy: the root `_UIHostingView<MainTabView>` had **exactly one** subview,
the platform view host. On iOS 26+ the tab view is **UIKit's own**
(`UIKitAdaptableTabView`), and it swallows the bottom inset's content whole —
the state was right all the time and the pixels were never produced.

The bar is now an **overlay** in the `ZStack` — the same shape the docked
player already proved works — and its bottom padding is the tab bar's height
**measured from the live window** (`MainTabView.tabBarHeightInWindow()`), not
guessed: on iOS 26+ the bar is a `_UIBottomTabBarGroupView` (83 pt) with a
floating pill inside it (62 pt), and the home indicator's safe area sits
*below* the pill. The bar therefore rests directly **above** the pill and never
covers it — the user's *"the minimise bar should be not cover the main tab bar
it saty uper side of the tab bar"*.

The bar itself is YouTube's: the channel's picture and its name, ▶/⏸, ⏭ and ✕.
⏭ plays the **next channel of the group the current one belongs to**
(`PlaylistStore.channels(inGroupOf:)`) — the same order the grid plays in.

**A swipe down puts the player away** — the docked player's picture and the
full-screen player both take the gesture, and both call the same rule
(`MiniPlayerSwipe.shouldMinimize`): the drag has to go **down** and further
down than sideways, so a sideways scroll never dismisses the player.

**The voice search crashed the app.** *"app crashing when i use mic feature
where mic feature is working but app crash."* The microphone worked — the
transcript arrived — and the app died with it. `SFSpeechRecognizer` calls its
result handler on **its own queue**, and the handler published
`recognizedText`, closed the search sheet and started the player **from that
queue**: *"Publishing changes from background threads are not allowed"* — a
fatal error with **no crash report** (it is a trap, not a signal), which is
why the crash logs had nothing to show. The transcript now crosses to the main
actor before anything touches the app's state
(`VoiceSearchManager.handleTranscript` — a `nonisolated` seam that hops through
`Task { @MainActor in }`), which is also what makes it testable: **AC117**
proves the transcript arrives on the main thread.

**A portrait video was drawn as a landscape one.** *"my iphone has a local
video that streams in portrait mode but in the drive tv i got black parts on
both side."* Every surface asked the player to **fit** the picture
(`.resizeAspect`), so a 9:16 video stood in the middle of the landscape car's
screen with a black bar on each side. `VideoGravityPolicy.gravity(for:)` reads
the item's own `presentationSize` — the size **with** the preferred transform,
so a rotated video is measured the way it is shown — and fills the frame only
when the picture is taller than it is wide. A landscape channel keeps
`.resizeAspect`, because a logo at the edge of the picture must not be cropped
away (**AC118**).

### 1.21 The twelfth pass — the ad blocker and the Plex account

Two defects the user found on the phone: *"the ad blocker is also blocking
captcha of the sonyLIV login (find issue and tell me the truth) FIx that"* and
*"on chrome side the status show succesfull but on the mobile side it show me
'No Plex media server found on this account' so fix"*.

#### The truth about the SonyLIV captcha

**No rule of the blocker ever matched the reCAPTCHA addresses.** That was
measured, not guessed: every one of the 237 host patterns was searched against
the captcha's own URLs (`www.google.com/recaptcha/enterprise.js`,
`…/recaptcha/api2/anchor`, `…/recaptcha/api2/bframe`,
`www.gstatic.com/recaptcha/…`), and **not one pattern contained "captcha"**.
A real `WKWebView`, loaded with the app's own compiled rules, still produced
`window.grecaptcha: object` on Google's reCAPTCHA demo page.

The page broke for four other reasons — and all four are real. Every
claim below was **measured** against a real `WKWebView` on a local server,
because the first version of this fix was written from the *documentation* and
was wrong in three places:

1. **The block rules never stopped an ad at all.** They named the ad hosts
   with `if-domain`, and `if-domain` reads the **top document's** host — not
   the host of the request. Measured: a rule for the host of an image never
   stopped that image, and a rule for the host of the *page* stopped the whole
   page. So on a real page the ad resources were never blocked by those rules,
   and on an ad host's own page **everything** was blocked. The rules now
   match the **host of the request** with a filter that is anchored to it —
   `^https?://([^/]+\.)?<host>(:[0-9]+)?/` — so a host that is only *mentioned*
   later in the URL (a path, a query) can never match. That is what stops
   Google's own collector, which carries
   `&scrsrc=www.googletagmanager.com` in its query string (**AC120**, **AC121**).

2. **The rule that hides the ad boxes ran on every page *and every frame*.**
   A captcha badge, a payment form and a consent banner all live in a
   **third-party frame**, and a rule that reaches into a frame can hide the
   thing the user came for. The rule is now `load-type: ["first-party"]` — the
   page itself and nothing else — and it excepts the captcha and identity
   providers (`google.com`, `gstatic.com`, `recaptcha.net`, `hcaptcha.com`,
   `cloudflare.com`, `arkoselabs.com`, `funcaptcha.com`) with `unless-domain`
   (**AC122**).

   `"top-frame"` is **not** a load type WebKit knows: it knows `"first-party"`
   and `"third-party"` and nothing else, and **one value it does not know
   fails the compilation of the whole list**. The rule list was therefore
   **dead** — the blocker did nothing at all, and the app's log said
   *"Rule list compilation failed: Invalid string in the trigger flags array."*
   There is a test now that compiles the list the app really hands over and
   fails when WebKit refuses it (**AC134**).

3. **The exemption killed the whole list.** It was written as
   `if-top-url: "*://<site>/*"`, and WebKit refuses a glob that starts with
   `*://` (*"Internal error in YARR"*) — one bad rule and the **entire** list
   fails to compile, so the blocker stopped blocking the moment a site was
   exempted. The exemption is now `if-domain`, which reads the top document's
   host — exactly what an exemption needs — with the `www.` twin named as
   well, because `if-domain` matches **exactly** (**AC123**).

4. **There was no way out, and the tag manager was blocked.** `isEnabled`
   existed and was **never read** — the "Ad Shield" sheet was a read-only
   notice. The sheet is a real control now, with a master switch and a
   **per-site switch**: a site that needs a captcha to log in is let through
   (an `ignore-previous-rules` rule that comes **last**, so it wins), while
   the rest of the web stays blocked (**AC123–AC125**). The site name is
   checked before it is used — `URL(string: "https://*.evil.com/x")` parses,
   and such a name would let **every** site through (**AC133**). And
   `googletagmanager.com` is not an ad host — it is the pipe through which a
   site publishes its **own** configuration, and SonyLIV's sign-in page took
   its login-and-tray setup from there; it is not blocked any more
   (**AC126**).

A fifth thing was found on the way: **the compiled rules are cached under
their identifier.** WebKit keeps the compiled list and the app asks for it *by
name* before it compiles anything, so the list compiled from the **old** rules
was found and used and the new rules were **never even compiled**. The
identifier is now `DrivePlayAdBlockerRulesV8`.

And a sixth: **the rules apply to the *next* load, and they are compiled in the
background.** The page was reloaded the moment the switch was flipped — with
the *old* rules — and the switch looked like it had done nothing. The pages
are loaded again **after** the compile, by the one place that knows the rules
changed. (That a recompile under the *same* identifier really does replace the
rules was measured, with a real `WKWebView` against a local server: rules A
stopped a page from loading, rules B compiled under the same identifier let it
through again.)

And one more fact, read out of SonyLIV's own bundle: the captcha is a **feature
flag**. The sign-in page decides on `enableReCAPTCHA` — it is `false` in the
page state today, and `true` for the accounts SonyLIV has switched over. When
the flag is on, the page injects
`<script id="reCaptchaScript" src="https://www.google.com/recaptcha/enterprise.js?render=…">`
itself, and the OTP request is sent as **API version 4.0** with the captcha's
token — so a captcha that is hidden or that never loads does not show an
error: the request simply goes out **without** the token and the account is
refused. That is the shape of the failure the user saw.

#### The proof, with the app's own rules

The rules the app really hands over were compiled by **WebKit** and run
against a real page in a real `WKWebView` (the app's own
`AdBlockerEngine.makeRules`, a local server, and the real internet for the
ad host):

```
pageAd:            none      ← the page's own ad boxes are hidden
thirdPartyFrameAd: block     ← the frame's ad box is NOT hidden (the captcha)
adRequested:       false     ← the ad host's request never happened
captchaRequested:  true      ← the reCAPTCHA script was requested
collectorRequested:true      ← Google's collector, which only *mentions* the
                                tag manager, was requested
```

That is the whole truth of the captcha in one line: **the captcha was never
blocked, the ads are blocked now, and the page's own requests are left alone.**
The rule list **compiles** — 237 rules, 238 with an exemption, 239 with two —
and a test asks WebKit to compile it on the phone (**AC134**), because one
value WebKit does not know kills every rule at once.

#### The truth about the Plex account

*"No Plex media server found on this account"* was used for **four** different
failures. The account was linked in Chrome ("Account Linked"), so the link
itself was fine — but the app asked the wrong question and read the answer
wrongly:

1. **The relay address was never asked for.** `GET /api/v2/resources` was
   called with `includeHttps=1` and **without** `includeRelay=1`. The relay
   address is the `*.plex.direct` address of the server — the one a phone that
   is not on the server's own network can reach. Without it the answer holds a
   server with **no usable address at all**, and the account looks empty.
   This is the most likely cause of the user's symptom (**AC131**).

2. **One odd device emptied the whole list.** Every field of a device was
   required, so a single resource that left one out made the answer
   unreadable — and an unreadable answer was reported as *"this account has no
   media server"*. Every field has a default now (**AC127**).

3. **`provides` came in two shapes.** plex.tv answers `"server"` *and*
   `["server"]`; a decoder that knows one of them reads **nothing**
   (**AC128**).

4. **The status was never read.** A refused account (401) answers with a JSON
   **object**, which does not decode as a list — so it was swallowed into
   "no servers". The status is read before the body now, and a refused
   account says *"plex.tv refused the account (HTTP 401) — sign in again"*,
   which is a different message with a different answer (**AC132**).

And one more: the fallback for a device that says nothing about itself used
*every* resource, so the account's **own phone** could pass for the server. A
device that says it is a **player** is never the server (**AC129**).

Three complaints in one round: *"video not automatic starting to car display
and laggy"*, *"when i start youtube from car display so it start on mobile
not on the car display and when i try to type any think in serach bar so it
crash the browser tab and redirect to home screen"*, *"the compase shows

**4. Nobody was asked to start the share.** iOS always asks the **user**

**5. The browser tab died, and the app went back to the home screen.** There

### 1.23 The fourteenth pass — the compass that was still wrong, and not smooth (AC143–AC149)

*"compass still not working properly and its not smooth"*.

**First, a wrong conclusion of my own.** The capture I took said the heading
callback never fired: no `didUpdateHeading`, no log line, nothing. It was the
**capture** that was broken — `idevicesyslog` had printed *"Waiting for
device … to become available"*, exited, and written **three** lines, and a
three-line capture cannot prove a callback is absent. The same capture did
hold two `didUpdateHeading` entries, and `xcrun devicectl device process
launch --console` shows the compass working outright (*"heading 266°, rose
−266°"*). **A capture is evidence only when it has content** — and the
device's own log line is the ground truth:

```
didUpdateHeading: magneticHeading 266.75 trueHeading 267.05 accuracy 27.58
                   headingMode 0 x +32.265 y -19.425 z -7.843
```

**1. The heading was out by 27° and was shown as a direction.**
`headingAccuracy` is the heading's **error**, and 27.58° is not a direction.
A phone lying flat has no usable horizontal field to read a heading from, so
its error grows into the tens of degrees — and a needle that follows such a
reading points the wrong way, which is **worse** than the GPS's course. A
reading the compass is out by more than twenty degrees is refused now, the
course takes over, and the panel says **GPS COURSE** instead of pretending
the compass knows (**AC147**). The compass says whether it is sure, and the
screen says so (**AC149**).

Note what the same two lines say about the *noise*: the readings were
**1.06°** apart. The noise is small; the **error** is large. A filter cannot
fix that one — only a gate can.

**2. The smoothing counted readings, not seconds.** "Close a third of the
gap per reading" is not a filter: the magnetometer delivers several times a
second and the GPS once a second, so it smooths one of them and not the
other. And CoreLocation delivers in **bursts** — the two readings above were
measured a second apart and handed over **128 ms** apart, so the needle took
two steps in a fraction of a second: the jolt the user called "not smooth".
The filter now runs on a **clock** (`1 − e^(−dt/τ)`), so the needle moves by
how much *time* has passed, and the same readings move it less when they
arrive together (**AC148**). The seam takes the reading's time for exactly
this reason — a test that cannot drive the clock cannot pin this down.

The clock is the moment a reading **arrived**, not the reading's own stamp:
what the user sees is when the needle moved, not when the field was
measured. And the clock only ever moves **forward** — a reading that arrives
late (a buffered one whose own stamp is older than the reading already
applied) no longer moves the needle and no longer drags the clock back,
which would make the *next* reading close a gap that never happened
(**AC148** pins both).

**3. A red needle said "north" while the letters said something else.** The
needle is **fixed** (it points the way the car faces) but it was painted like
a magnetic compass's needle — red for north — so it pointed up for ever while
the rose turned under it, and the rose's own red **N** went round the dial.
That is two north markers that disagree, which is what "shows completely
wrong direction" meant. The dial is a **heading indicator** now: the card
turns, one green marker stays at the top, and red is the card's **N** alone.

**4. The rose spun the long way round at north.** The rotation was wrapped
into `0..<360`, so every time the heading crossed north the rose turned
**358°** backwards. The rotation is continuous now (**AC144**), and the
heading is smoothed (**AC143**). The course is used only while the car is
**moving** (**AC146**) and an unknown heading (−1) is refused without
killing the course fallback (**AC145**).

### 1.24 The security review that the hook could not finish (AC150–AC151)

The **automated** commit-time security review **timed out** on this tree — its
own log says *"The read operation timed out"* and *"the model's reply could not
be read"*, so the commit was **not marked reviewed**. The review was therefore
run by hand over the whole uncommitted diff. Six findings; three are fixed
here, three are written down instead (with the reason).

**Fixed**

1. **The screen stream's token was 32 bits wide.** `Shared/LiveScreenStream.swift`
   built the token every path of the stream carries from
   `String(UUID().uuidString.prefix(8))` — eight hex characters, 2³² guesses.
   The stream is the phone's **screen**, served over the local network, and
   the token is the only thing that keeps it private; on hotel Wi-Fi, 2³²
   guesses is a weekend's work. The token is **128 bits** now — the whole
   UUID (**AC150**).
2. **A failed stream logged the account's token.** `PlaybackManager` logged
   `String(describing: itm.error)`, and an `NSError`'s description carries the
   URL that failed — which for Plex, Jellyfin and Xtream is where the token,
   the API key and the password live. The device's log is readable by anything
   attached to the phone (Xcode's console, `idevicesyslog`, a sysdiagnose
   upload). The log now keeps the **host and the file name** and nothing else
   (**AC151**).
3. **The browser logged the URL the user typed.** `BrowserTabManager` printed
   the raw destination, and a pasted `https://user:password@host/` puts the
   password in the log. Same redaction as above (**AC151**).

**Written down, not fixed — and why**

4. **The relay's control channel is unauthenticated.** The broadcast extension
   announces its stream as `<port> <token>` on the fixed loopback port
   **47311**, and the app takes **any** announcement: a second app on the same
   phone could announce a stream of its own and have it played — and, since
   this round's diff hands the share to the car, played **on the car's
   display**. The app and the extension share **no secret** (they live in
   different processes and share no container — an App Group would need another
   Apple-granted capability, and `AGENTS.md` says to ask before that). Closing
   it properly means either an App Group, or the user's own confirmation
   before an announced stream is shown. **Neither is a change to make
   unasked**, so it is written down.
5. **A source whose secrets the Keychain refuses is written to
   `UserDefaults` in the clear.** When `SecItemAdd` fails with
   `errSecMissingEntitlement` (-34018), `PlaylistStore` deliberately skips the
   redaction and keeps the username, the password and the token in the plist.
   It is a documented fallback for a mis-provisioned build, and it is
   plaintext-at-rest for a user secret — a trade the app has already made
   deliberately. It stays.
6. **Neither `WKWebView` has a navigation policy.** The scheme is constrained
   at the two entry points (`BrowserTabManager` and the CarPlay browser coerce
   anything that is not `http(s)` to `https://`), so `file://` and
   `javascript:` cannot be typed — but a **redirect** or a link tap is not
   checked at all, and the guard does not live where the navigation happens.
   Today this is low severity: the app registers no script message handler, no
   user script and no file-access preference, so a loaded page has no bridge
   into the app. A `decidePolicyFor` backstop in both web views is the right
   fix; it is a change to the browser's own code path and is written down
   rather than slipped into this round.

**Clean**

- **Injection — clean.** No SQL anywhere; the only `NSRegularExpression` uses
  a literal pattern; no remote-provided string is ever used as a file name or
  a path component.
- **The content blocker's site names — clean.** `AdBlockerEngine.siteKey(for:)`
  returns `nil` for anything that is not a plain host name, the exemption set
  is re-validated on the way in **and** on the way out, so a rule can only
  ever name a literal host.
- **The CarPlay entitlement — clean.** Nothing in the code writes, patches or
  simulates an entitlement; the device build refuses to sign without the
  Apple-granted profile.
- **The stream server — clean on the two questions asked.** The token **is**
  checked on every request, and there is **no** path traversal: the server
  serves in-memory fragments by an `Int`-parsed index and never touches the
  file system.

### 1.25 The sixteenth pass — the whole browser mechanism (AC152–AC157)

The report the user gave was *"when i try to write anything inside keyboard of
the brower so the browserscreen close and redirect to home"* — and, a day
earlier, *"it crash the browser tab and redirect tom home screen"* — plus
*"the browser added that not working any more"* on the car's side. **Six**
separate faults were behind them. The two that actually killed the browser
were found by reading the **device's own log**, not the app's: the capture
holds `GPUProcessProxy::gpuProcessExited: reason=Crash` and four
`WebProcessProxy::processDidTerminateOrFailedToLaunch: reason=Crash` — and
**no crash report at all** from the app, because nothing in the app crashed.

**1. The ad blocker tore the content process down under the page (AC152).**
`BrowserTabManager.load()` called `AdBlockerEngine.apply(to:)` on **every**
navigation, and `apply(to:)` did `removeAllContentRuleLists()` + `add()` on
the live web view's content controller. A `WKWebViewConfiguration` is copied
when the web view is made, but its `userContentController` is a **shared
reference** — so the rule store of the content process was torn down while
the page was still loading. That is what the `reason=Crash` lines are. The
rules are applied **once** now, and `apply(to:)` says whether it changed
anything, so the caller knows whether the page has to be loaded again.

**2. The crash handler emptied the address — and an empty address IS the
start page (AC155).** `webViewWebContentProcessDidTerminate` set
`urlString = ""`, which is the browser's own *"redirect to home screen"*. The
address is **kept** now and the tab says *"The page crashed"*; the page is
reloaded **once** and not in a loop (a loop loads the page that killed the
process again, kills it again, and the watchdog takes the app down).

**3. The address bar showed the page's *previous* address.** The mirror of
the active tab's URL read `tab.urlString` inside `objectWillChange` — and
`objectWillChange` fires **before** the value changes, so the bar was always
one change behind. It now subscribes to the `$published` publisher, which
carries the **new** value.

**4. The phone's two search boxes were one text field (AC156, AC157).** The
address bar at the bottom and the start page's own search box shared one
`@State` binding, so what was typed in one appeared in the other, and the
bar was filled over the text the user had already typed — which is why the
typing *"did nothing"*. They are two fields now, each with its own text, and
the bar is only filled **while it is empty**.

**5. The car's search could never work.** `presentSearch()` **presented** a
`CPSearchTemplate`, and `presentTemplate` accepts only an action sheet, an
alert and voice control — so the presentation failed every time and the
Search button did nothing at all. It is **pushed** onto the stack now.

**6. The address bar had no way back (AC153, AC154).** A `CPSearchTemplate`
does not conform to `CPBarButtonProviding` — it cannot carry a navigation bar
button — so the way back is a row of the search's own list (*"Back to
sites"*, *"Back to channels"*). The channel search also rebuilt thirty rows,
and thirty pictures at the car's scale, for **every keystroke**; the rows are
built once and filtered afterwards, in a bounded cache.

**Verified**

- The **whole suite on the physical phone**: 180 tests, 7 skipped, **0
  failures** — the six new ones included, run on their own to prove they are
  not skipped.
- The **AC trace** is mechanical: **157 SPEC rows ↔ 157 test names**, the same
  set.
- **Not verified end to end**: the CarPlay Simulator run. The phone's **USB
  link dropped** during the session (`devicectl` reports
  `Transport Type: localNetwork`) and Xcode 27's CarPlay Simulator attaches
  to physical devices **over USB only** — its *Sessions* menu says *"No
  Devices Connected"*. The templates themselves are covered by the tests
  above; the last mile needs the cable back in.

### 1.26 The seventeenth pass — the page the car asked for never came to the phone (AC158)

Reading the browser's chain end to end — the car picks a URL → the page opens
on the phone → the phone's screen share carries it to the car — turned up one
more fault, and it is the one that makes the car's browser **dead in the
middle**: the last link was missing.

**7. The page the car asked for stayed on a tab nobody was looking at.**
`MainTabView` kept the selected tab in a `@State` **inside the view** — so
**nothing outside the view could change it**. The car's browser opened the page
in the phone's browser tab (`BrowserTabManager.loadURLInActiveTab`) and set
`pendingCarPage`, and the phone **stayed on the tab it was on**. The share the
car needs captures **the phone's screen**, so the car was waiting for a share
of the *wrong* screen — and the phone's own *"the car is waiting for this
page"* banner, which carries the button that starts the share, sat on the

### 1.27 The eighteenth pass — the car's own browser, and the keyboard that ate the key presses (AC159–AC164)

Two complaints, one cause and one truth.

**8. The car's browser was a remote control for the iPhone.**
The Browser tab is a `CPSearchTemplate` — a text field and a list of links.
That is not a shortcoming of the app: **`CarPlay.framework` has no web
template at all** (iOS 27: `CPActionSheetTemplate`, `CPAlertTemplate`,
`CPContactTemplate`, `CPGridTemplate`, `CPInformationTemplate`,
`CPListTemplate`, `CPMapTemplate`, `CPNowPlayingTemplate`,

there. Where the system refuses — on a phone it answers *"The current device
does not support multiple scenes."*, which the app's own log carries — the old
path survives and the page runs on the phone (AC159–AC162).

**9. The car's keyboard clicked the row behind it.**
`CPSearchTemplate` has **no keyboard of its own** — the header carries
`delegate` and nothing else — so the keyboard that comes up is the **car's**,
and it floats over the **bottom of the list**. The address bar's results were
the **whole catalogue of sites** — seventeen rows with big tiles — so a row was
**always** under the keyboard, and a key press landed on that row instead of
the key (the user's *"the keyboard clicks the row behind it"*). Measured on the
simulator's 1920×1080 car screen: the keyboard covers the bottom ~27 %, and a
result row's band is **full width**, so a key press at (391, 464) sits inside
the Pluto TV row's band.

The app's only lever is what it puts **under** the keyboard, so the address
bar's list is **short** now: the way back, what the user typed, and at most
two matches (AC163). The whole catalogue stays in the Browser tab's own list,
one tap away. And a site answers to its **name** as well as to its address —
"HBO Max" is nowhere in "max.com", and the name is what the user reads on the
row (AC164).

## 4. The platform constraints that are still there

1. **CarPlay draws the video, not the app.** The signal is
   `CPListItem.playbackConfiguration` (`preferredPresentation: .video`, iOS
   26.4+); the entitlement `com.apple.developer.carplay-video` is Apple-granted
   and now **is** in the profile.
2. **iOS refuses to record the screen while CarPlay is connected**
   (`RPRecordingErrorCarPlay`, -5813). The car's row therefore waits for a
   **broadcast** (the WhatsApp-style share), not for a capture.
3. **ScreenCaptureKit cannot replace the broadcast on iOS**: `SCShareableContent`
   is unavailable on iOS, and the only iOS picker captures the app's own screen.
4. **The whole ReplayKit broadcast family is "no longer supported" in iOS 27**
   except the picker, which is still there and is what the app uses.
5. **A CarPlay app cannot show a web view.** The Browser tab is a list, and its
   address bar — the only CarPlay chrome with a text field — is **pushed** onto
   the stack, because the tab bar rejects a search template outright. What it
   opens runs in the app's own browser on the phone, and the screen share
   carries it to the car — the closest compliant interaction.
6. **No public API picks an AirPlay route.** The app can *offer* the picture
   to the car (the car is an AirPlay display) and the system reports the route
   back, but choosing the car's display is the system's own list — or, with a
   car connected, no choice at all.
7. **`UIBackgroundModes: audio`** normally wants the
   `com.apple.developer.avfoundation.multitasking` entitlement, which the
   CarPlay entitlements files do not carry. Changing the entitlements risks the
   signing chain, so it is reported, not changed.
8. **CarPlay trims a template silently** (`maximumItemCount`,
   `maximumSectionCount`), so every category needs its own "Show all N" row —
   which is why the Home tab has one.
9. **A connected car can lock the phone's microphone for every app.** The
   system's speech daemon holds the CarPlay route, the activation is refused
   with `'!pri'` and the input with `'what'` — for *any* app, with any
   category and any wait. The voice search cannot fight it; it can only give
   the player's session back and say so.

## 5. Needs a human

* **Replug the cable, or unlock the phone** — a locked device shows as
  `unavailable` and cannot be driven. This round the phone dropped off the
  USB bus entirely while the compass work was being finished
  (`xcrun devicectl list devices` → *unavailable*,
  `system_profiler SPUSBDataType` → no iPhone at all), and the device suite
  sat queued for hours behind a cable that was never plugged back in. **It
  did not need the cable**: a paired phone off the USB bus is still
  reachable over **Wi-Fi** — `devicectl device info lockState` answers and
  `xcodebuild -showdestinations` lists it — and the suite ran on it that way
  (174 tests, 0 failures, 15:12). **Test the link, not the word** in the
  device list: a waiter that waits for the literal word *"connected"* waits
  for ever over Wi-Fi, and *"disconnected"* contains *"connected"*. What is
  still worth a human is **the phone locking itself** — a locked screen
  backgrounds the app and the test times out — so the run waits it out
  (30 s apart, eight times) rather than reporting a failure.

* **Two security decisions are yours to make** (§1.24). The relay's control
  channel — the loopback port **47311** on which the broadcast extension
  announces its stream — is **unauthenticated**, so a second app on the same
  phone could announce a stream of its own and have it played, now also on
  the car's display. Closing it needs either an **App Group** (another
  Apple-granted capability — the app and the extension share no container
  today) or the **user's own confirmation** before an announced stream is
  shown. And neither of the app's web views has a **navigation policy**: the
  scheme is constrained where the address is entered, but a redirect or a
  link tap is not checked, so a `decidePolicyFor` backstop belongs in both.
  Both are written down rather than slipped in.

Everything else in this document is done. The **device suite** runs on the
phone — the second round's twenty tests (AC70–AC89), the microphone
(AC67/AC68), the third pass's two (AC90/AC91), the sixth pass's three
(AC98–AC100), the seventh pass's three (AC101–AC103) and the eighth pass's
seven (AC104–AC110) and the tenth pass's one (AC111) are all executed there:
**157 passed, 1 failed (AC32), 7 skipped** in the twelfth pass's run.

Three things on the car need a human eye and a real head unit:

* **The four tabs** — the system paints the bar and the selection marker, so
  the dark segmented appearance is the system's own; the app only supplies the
  titles, the icons and the artwork. That has to be seen on a car.
* **The picture on the car** — the video surface comes from
  `CPPlaybackConfiguration(preferredPresentation: .video)` and the
  Apple-granted `com.apple.developer.carplay-video` entitlement. The
  entitlement is in the profile; the surface itself can only be confirmed on
  a car that is paired with the phone. What to look for in the device log:
  the route evaluation must stop saying *"Local Playback = 1"* and name the
  car's endpoint, and `isExternalPlaybackActive` must become true. Then, on
  the phone: the switch must say **"Sending to Car…"** and turn into
  **"Car connected"** when the system confirms it, the phone's own picture
  must go away **at that moment** (not before) and come back when
  **"Play on Phone"** is tapped.
* **The Browser tab** — the address bar is the system's own search field,
  pushed onto the stack. Whether the system seeds a search template's results
  when it appears is the system's business: if it does not, the sites appear as
  soon as the field is touched. And the **one tap that matters now** is
  opening DrivePlay from the car's app list: that is what proved the crash, so
  it is what proves the fix — the app must come up on the car and stay up.
* **The keyboard on the car's display** — the one thing the app cannot check
  for itself. The keyboard is the **car's**, it floats over the bottom of the
  list, and the app's only lever is what it puts under it: the address bar's
  results are short now (the way back, what was typed, and at most two
  matches) so that no row sits under the keyboard. What to try on the car:
  open the Browser tab → *"Search or type a URL"* → **type** — the characters
  must land in the search field and the row behind must **not** be selected.
  Then type a site's **name** ("HBO Max", "Prime Video", "Sony LIV") and the
  site must come up as a result. A real head unit draws its own keyboard, so
  the overlap may look different there — but a row that steals the key
  presses is the app's fault on any car, because the row is the app's.
* **The microphone, spoken to** — the recorder now takes the microphone
  reliably and the device test proves it, but *what it hears* is Apple's
  speech recognizer: it needs a real voice in a real room. The test proves
  the microphone opens, the route comes up and the recorder runs — not that a
  name is understood. And while a car is connected the system may refuse the
  microphone **for every app** (§1.15) — the app then says the microphone
  could not be opened and gives the player's sound back; the microphone
  itself is the system's to hand out.
